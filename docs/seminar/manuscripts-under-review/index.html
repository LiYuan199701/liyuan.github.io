BPAR · chaotic time series

What makes a time series chaotic?

Two Mackey-Glass trajectories and a lower strip showing peak separation amplification. Use the time slider to inspect the simulation.
initial gap δ₀

A chaotic time series follows deterministic nonlinear rules, yet nearby initial histories can separate rapidly. Sensitive dependence limits long-horizon predictability even without external noise.

ẋ(t) = 0.2x(t−17) / [1 + x(t−17)10] − 0.1x(t)

x(t) = current state; 17 = delay; 0.2 = delayed feedback; 0.1 = decay.

δ₀ = ‖φ̃−φ‖∞;   Δt = x̃(t)−x(t)

φ, φ̃ = initial histories; δ₀ = their initial gap; Δₜ = separation at time t.

At = |Δt|/δ₀;   Atpeak = max0≤s≤t |Δs|/δ₀

Aₜ = separation now in initial-gap units; Aₜpeak = largest ratio observed through t.

current amplification 1.0× · peak 1.0×

Current = separation now; peak = largest normalized separation so far. Not a probability or a Lyapunov-exponent estimate.

BPAR · approximation theory

Bernstein approximation theory — fixed basis, flexible lag effects.

degree m = 3
4 basis units RMS — max gap —

Bk,m(z) = C(m,k)zk(1−z)m−k

z ∈ [0,1] is the scaled lag; m is the degree; k indexes the basis; C(m,k) is the binomial coefficient.

(ℬmf)(z) = Σk=0m f(k/m) Bk,m(z)

ℬₘf blends the sampled values f(k/m) using nonnegative basis weights that sum to one.

‖ℬmf − f‖∞ → 0  for f ∈ C[0,1]

As m increases, the largest approximation error anywhere on [0,1] tends to zero.

BPAR replaces sampled values with learned coefficients:

gj(z) ≈ Σk=0m wj,kBk,m(z)

gⱼ(z) = nonlinear response of lag j; wⱼ,ₖ = learned coefficient for basis k.

Inference details

variational route (both models)

ℒ = 𝔼q[log p(y | θ)] − KL(q(θ) ∥ p(θ))

ℒ = variational objective; q(θ) = approximate posterior; p(θ) = prior; KL penalizes departure from the prior.

Factorized Gaussian q · reparameterization θ = µ + softplus(ρ)·ε · closed-form KL · BAMIC: one Monte Carlo draw per batch.

conjugate route (AR-BPAR)

θ | σ² ∼ 𝒩(m₀, σ²V₀)
σ² ∼ InvGamma(a₀, b₀)

θ = regression coefficients; σ² = noise variance; m₀,V₀,a₀,b₀ = prior hyperparameters.

The linear-in-coefficients likelihood gives a closed-form Normal–Inverse-Gamma posterior; prior variances decay with lag j and basis index k.
